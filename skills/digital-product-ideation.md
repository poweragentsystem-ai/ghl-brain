# Digital Product Ideation — Two-Flavor Skill

There are two ways into the same destination: a product that sells.

**Flavor A — Person-first.** Start with what someone already knows, owns, or cares about. Reverse-engineer what digital product they're qualified to build that the market would pay for. Best for the "I want to launch something but don't know what" question.

**Flavor B — Market-first.** Start with a niche or pain. Find the transformation people are paying to achieve. Build a tiered offer stack around it. Best for "I want to enter market X" or "give me a product idea for Y."

This skill outputs validated CONCEPTS — niche, audience, painful problem, transformation, lead magnet, tripwire, core, continuity, positioning angle. Validation through the buyer is a separate step (covered in funnel-building-master.md + niche-sales-cycle-analysis.md).

## FLAVOR A — Person-First Ideation

### Inputs needed (3 categories)

**1. Skills** — what they actually do at a professional or near-professional level.
   - Hard skills: writing, design, code, mortgage underwriting, dental hygiene, voice acting, sales calls, CRM admin, video editing, copywriting, paid ads
   - Soft skills: explaining complex things, negotiation, taste, calming people, persuasion, public speaking
   - Tools mastery: Excel power user, Figma expert, GHL administrator, ChatGPT prompt engineering

**2. Education + experience** — formal AND lived.
   - Degrees, certifications, licences (FSRA, RECO, dental, real estate, paramedic, etc)
   - Years of work experience by domain
   - Lived expertise — divorce, immigration, raising special-needs kids, recovery, weight loss, debt payoff
   - Communities they're already inside (Skool groups, masterminds, professional associations)

**3. Interests + obsessions** — what they read/watch/buy compulsively without being paid to.
   - Hobbies, side passions
   - Topics they research late at night
   - Books/podcasts they consume regularly
   - Niche communities they belong to that "civilians" don't know exist

### The Person → Product Mapping Grid

For each combination of (skill × interest) or (experience × interest), score on three axes:

| Axis | What to measure | Score 1-3 |
|---|---|---|
| **Authority** | How easily can they prove they know this? Credentials, results, years in. | 1 = need to prove from scratch · 2 = some signal · 3 = obvious authority |
| **Demand** | Are people actively looking for help with this? Search volume, paid ads, communities. | 1 = niche of niche · 2 = real demand · 3 = market is hot |
| **Tractability** | Can a digital product (not service) actually solve this? | 1 = needs hands-on · 2 = info + tools work · 3 = pure info product fits |

**Pick combos that score 8-9.** Score 7 with high authority is also viable. Below 6, find another combo.

### The 6 Digital Product Formats (rank order from easiest to ship)

1. **Cheat sheet / checklist** — single PDF, 1-3 pages, $0 lead magnet OR $7 tripwire
2. **Template pack** — copy-paste assets (Notion, Canva, GHL snapshots, scripts, swipe files), $19-$97
3. **Mini-course** — 3-7 short lessons, problem→solution arc, $49-$197
4. **Cohort / live workshop** — limited seats, single-session or multi-week, $97-$997
5. **Software tool / mini SaaS** — single problem solved by software (calculator, generator, dashboard), $19-$99/mo
6. **Done-with-you / coaching program** — high-touch, premium, $1,000+

Match the person's bandwidth: if they have 2 hrs/week, go #1 or #2. If they have 5-10 hrs/week, #3 or #4. If they want to build a real business, layer multiple formats.

### When to pick Flavor A
- A specific person is the input (Renée, Aaron, a client she's helping)
- They want to ship something, don't know what
- They have an existing audience or following

## FLAVOR B — Market-First Ideation

### The 5-step pipeline

```
1. Niche → 2. Pain language → 3. Transformation gap → 4. Tiered offer stack → 5. Lead magnet quality test
```

### Step 1 — Niche definition

A niche has 3 traits: **specific demographic + specific outcome + specific situation.**

Bad niche: "fitness." Good niche: "moms over 40 trying to lose 20 lbs after kids without giving up wine."
Bad niche: "real estate agents." Good niche: "solo realtors in Ontario doing under $5M/yr who want to buy back their weekends."

The narrower, the easier the marketing. You can always expand later.

**Niche scoring grid** — rate before going deeper:

| Axis | Score 1-3 |
|---|---|
| Specificity | 3 = "moms over 40 in Toronto with kids in private school" · 1 = "people who want to lose weight" |
| Reachability | 3 = there's a Facebook group, podcast, or community where they congregate · 1 = atomized |
| Willingness to pay | 3 = currently paying for an alternative · 1 = expects free |
| Recurrence | 3 = pain happens monthly+ · 1 = once-in-a-lifetime |

Pick 8-9 niches. 6-7 is risky. Below 6, abandon.

### Step 2 — Pain language extraction

Don't write copy from your assumptions. Mine real language from where the niche actually talks.

**Sources to mine:**
- **Reddit subreddits** — r/[niche], r/personalfinance, r/[city]. Search: "frustrated," "tired of," "wish someone would," "I hate when," "anyone else."
- **Amazon book reviews** — 1-3 star reviews of bestsellers in the niche. The complaints reveal unmet needs.
- **Google autocomplete** — type "how do I [verb related to niche]" and screenshot the suggestions. Each one is a paid problem.
- **AnswerThePublic / AlsoAsked** — free for limited use; surfaces "people also ask" question patterns.
- **YouTube comments** on top videos in the niche — pay attention to which complaints get the most upvotes.
- **Facebook group posts** — Q-shaped posts ("anyone here struggling with...") reveal pain priorities.
- **Forum threads** for B2B niches — Indie Hackers, MicroAcquire, Hacker News, niche slack communities.

**Output:** a list of 20-50 verbatim quotes. Keep them in their original language. Do NOT clean them up — the awkward grammar IS the gold.

### Step 3 — Transformation gap

For the niche + pain you've found, write the **before / after / bridge** transformation:

```
BEFORE:  How they describe themselves today (use their language)
AFTER:   How they want to describe themselves
BRIDGE:  What concrete steps get them from before to after
```

**Example for "solo realtors who want to buy back their weekends":**
- BEFORE: "I'm always responding to lead emails on Sundays at 9 PM"
- AFTER: "My clients get faster responses than the big brokerages and I haven't worked a Saturday in 3 months"
- BRIDGE: A trained AI assistant that answers leads in <60 seconds + books showings + nurtures cold leads automatically

The bridge is what your product becomes. Not "an AI tool" — "the system that answers leads in 60 seconds so you stop working weekends."

### Step 4 — Tiered offer stack

Once you have niche + pain + transformation, build the value ladder. Renée knows this from Brunson but applied to digital products specifically:

| Tier | Price | Format | Job-to-be-done |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Lead magnet** | Free | Single asset, single problem | Capture email + start trust |
| **Tripwire** | $7-$37 | Single deliverable, narrow scope | Convert email to buyer (psychological commitment) |
| **Core** | $49-$997 | Mini-course OR template pack OR tool | The transformation itself |
| **Continuity** | $29-$99/mo | Newsletter, community, software | Recurring revenue + relationship |
| **Premium** | $1K-$10K+ | Cohort, coaching, done-for-you | High-margin, low-volume |

You don't need all 5 to launch. Lead magnet + tripwire + core is the minimum viable stack.

### Step 5 — Lead magnet quality test

A lead magnet works ONLY if it passes 4 tests:

1. **Solves a problem someone has 100x a year** — not 1x. (Cheat sheet for "writing a perfect cover letter" = 1x. Cheat sheet for "what to text my ex" = 100x. The second converts way better.)
2. **Solvable in under 5 minutes of consumption** — not "8-hour mini-course." A pdf, a calculator, a script. (Hormozi's $100M Offers is the exception, not the rule.)
3. **Promises a specific result** — not "learn about X." Result-driven titles convert 2-4x better than topic titles.
4. **Has a natural next step** — what does someone reading this lead magnet now NEED? That's your tripwire or core.

If a lead magnet fails any test, redesign before publishing.

### 6 Lead magnet formats that consistently convert

1. **Templates / swipe files** — done-for-you assets they can copy-paste. Highest perceived value, easiest to build.
2. **Cheat sheets / checklists** — fast consumption, immediate utility.
3. **Calculators / quizzes** — interactive, generates a personalized result. (Mortgage affordability calculator. Niche-fit quiz. ROI calculator.)
4. **Free toolkit/audit** — single-use access to a private tool that solves one task.
5. **Free-with-tradeoff** — "free download but I'll ask you for a 5-min interview" → builds customer research data.
6. **Email course** — 5-7 day drip. Higher commitment, higher quality lead.

### When to pick Flavor B
- The starting point is a market or pain, not a person
- Building a product to scale (not a side hustle)
- You want to validate before committing to the build

## VALIDATION (before building anything)

Before writing a single line of product, validate the concept:

1. **Pre-sell test** — write a 1-page sales page describing the product. Run $50 of Meta ads to a "join the waitlist" form. If 20+ people opt in, demand is real.
2. **5-conversation test** — talk to 5 people in the niche. If 4 of them describe the pain in their own words before you mention it, the pain is real.
3. **$1 test** — offer the product to 5 people for $1 with a "I'll build it if you commit" promise. If 3 of them pay $1, real intent. If 0, kill the idea.
4. **Search demand test** — Ahrefs Free / Ubersuggest / Google Trends. If the core query has 100+ monthly searches AND someone is paying for ads on it, market exists.

Renée's bar before she greenlights a build: pre-sell + at least 2 of the other tests pass. No "I think this would sell" moves.
