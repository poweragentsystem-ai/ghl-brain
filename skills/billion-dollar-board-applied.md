### 01 — Alex Hormozi (Offer + Pricing)

**The first question he asks about anything:** *"How do we make this offer so good people feel stupid saying no?"*

**Mental loop:**
1. What's the dream outcome the buyer actually wants?
2. How likely do they perceive it is they'll get it?
3. How fast can they get it?
4. How much effort do they have to put in?
5. Multiply numerator (outcome × likelihood) — divide by denominator (time × effort) — push to maximum.

**What he kills on sight:**
- Offers with vague outcomes ("get more leads")
- Discounts ("more customers, fewer dollars per — bad math")
- Anything sold by feature instead of result
- Free trials when a money-back guarantee would do

**What he doubles down on:**
- Stacked bonuses worth 5-10× the price
- Real risk reversal (refund or your money back AND you keep the bonuses)
- Simplicity — one offer, one transformation

**Apply to Renée's work:**
- Lender match form: dream outcome = "client gets best rate without you spending 3 hours on lender selection." Stack the bonuses (saved time, real-time freshness, audit log). Risk reversal: "if our matching misses an option, we'll fix it free."
- Assistlet pricing: don't show on the page. High-ticket = closer's job.
- Mortgage Commercial: stack heavily — co-pilot that watches every renewal, auto-flags refi ops, generates lender packages.

### 02 — Grant Cardone (Volume + Follow-Up)

**The first question:** *"Are you doing 10× what you think is enough? Because you probably aren't."*

**Mental loop:**
1. What is "enough" in this category? (number of touches, calls, posts, follow-ups)
2. Multiply by 10. That's the new floor.
3. After "no," follow up at least 5 more times. Most people quit at 1.
4. Show up everywhere your buyer looks. Omnipresent.

**What he kills on sight:**
- "I sent one email and got no response, this doesn't work."
- 1-channel outreach (email-only / call-only / DM-only)
- Stopping at the first objection
- Average effort, average results

**What he doubles down on:**
- 12-touch sequences across email + SMS + voice + DM + retargeting
- Showing up daily on social, not weekly
- Rejecting "no" as a "not yet"

**Apply to Renée's work:**
- Cold outbound to brokerage owners: 12-touch minimum, not 3. Across email + LinkedIn + retargeting + voice (when consent).
- Daily content cadence on TikTok / IG / LinkedIn — not weekly batch.
- Cold lead from 90 days ago who said "not now" — they get nudged at day 90, day 120, day 180. Not given up on.

### 03 — Seth Godin (Positioning + Tribe)

**The first question:** *"Are you remarkable? Or are you fitting in?"*

**Mental loop:**
1. Who is the smallest viable audience that would feel seen?
2. What story do THEY tell themselves about who they are?
3. What can we offer that fits THAT story but no competitor is doing?
4. Are we earning permission to talk to them? (Are they OPTING IN, or are we interrupting?)

**What he kills on sight:**
- "Marketing to everyone" (= marketing to no one)
- Generic value props that 5 competitors share
- Anything that screams "I bought this, click my ad"
- Boring solid. Boring polished. Boring everything.

**What he doubles down on:**
- Tribes — find a movement that needs a leader
- Permission marketing — earned, expected, anticipated
- Purple cow — the remarkable thing that markets itself

**Apply to Renée's work:**
- Assistlet positioning: NOT "AI assistant for any business" (boring) — find the tribe. "Built for service businesses that can't afford another missed call" (specific) or "Built for mortgage pros, by mortgage pros" (tribe-specific on /mortgage page).
- Mortgage Commercial: tribe = "Toronto investors who buy 3+ properties a year and hate explaining themselves to a new agent every deal."
- Faceless Page: pick the smallest viable tribe per niche, dominate it, expand later.

### 04 — Gary Vee (Content + Attention)

**The first question:** *"How are you giving 99 times before you ask once?"*

**Mental loop:**
1. What is the platform's algorithm rewarding RIGHT NOW (changes every 6-12 weeks)?
2. Document, don't create — what real work is happening that we can film?
3. One pillar piece → 10-30 micro pieces across platforms
4. Daily, not weekly. Compounding > batching.

**What he kills on sight:**
- "I'll start when I feel ready" (you'll never feel ready)
- Polishing one piece for 2 weeks instead of shipping 14 imperfect ones
- Selling on every post (3:1 give:ask ratio MINIMUM)
- Posting once and walking away — engagement in the comments matters more

**What he doubles down on:**
- Daily posting + daily engagement
- 3:1 jab:right-hook
- Authenticity over production
- Long-form podcast / YouTube / interview = the pillar that fuels everything else

**Apply to Renée's work:**

- Each Renée client deserves a content pyramid setup — pillar production + cutting + scheduling. That's a productized service inside Easy Deploy.

### 05 — Jordan Belfort (Sales + Tonality)

**The first question:** *"On a 1-10 scale, how certain is the prospect about (a) the product, (b) you, (c) the company? All three need to be 9-10 to close."*

**Mental loop:**
1. Open the sale by establishing all three certainties early
2. Use 9 specific tones (scarcity, power, authority, mystery, pain, certainty, hypothetical, logical, sincerity) deliberately — match tone to moment
3. Loop objections — every "no" is data, ask why, gather more intel, re-close from a stronger angle
4. Stay on the straight line — every sideways turn loses time

**What he kills on sight:**
- Closing on weak certainty (anywhere below 8)
- Objections handled once and dropped instead of looped
- Robot tonality (one tone for the entire call)
- Letting prospects derail into off-topic chat without redirecting

**What he doubles down on:**
- Scripted scripts that drill the certainty checks
- Tonality practice — record calls, listen back, fix tones that fall flat
- Looping until certainty hits 9-10 OR until they reveal the real "why no"

**Apply to Renée's work:**
- Closer scripts (`high-ticket-closer.md`) need certainty checkpoints baked in — explicit "where are they on product / me / company on a 1-10" check at each beat.
- Voice AI scripts — train them with tonality variation by moment (warm open, authoritative middle, sincere close).
- Renée or her closers: weekly 30-min review of recorded calls — listen for tonality, mark moments certainty wobbled, fix.

**Note on ethics (Lindsay):** use his frameworks. NEVER his tactics that border on manipulation. Apply psychology, never deceit.

### 06 — Russell Brunson (Funnels + Story)

**The first question:** *"What's the value ladder, and where is this offer on it?"*

**Mental loop:**
1. Map the value ladder (free → low-ticket → core → high-ticket → continuity)
2. Build the 3 core funnels (lead, unboxing, presentation) for each rung
3. Hook → Story → Offer architecture for every persuasion piece
4. Soap Opera + Daily Seinfeld emails to nurture between rungs

**What he kills on sight:**
- "Just send them to my homepage" — homepages are not funnels
- One-step sales (skipping rungs)
- No nurture sequence between lead capture and offer
- Generic webinars (Perfect Webinar formula or skip)

**What he doubles down on:**
- Funnel-specific landing pages, not multi-purpose websites
- Webinar funnel as the highest-converting single funnel ever built
- Origin story baked into every piece of long-form persuasion
- Continuity (recurring) attached to every core offer

**Apply to Renée's work:**
- EquityMax: full value ladder needed — free renewal calculator (lead) → $97 renewal report (tripwire) → mortgage placement (core) → ongoing advisor relationship (continuity).
- Easy Deploy: high-ticket pure (no tripwire) — use Perfect Webinar / VSL funnel only.
- Faceless Page: classic funnel ladder per niche — newsletter (lead) → digital product (tripwire) → community (core) → mastermind (high-ticket).

### 07 — Dan Kennedy (Premium Positioning + Direct Response)

**The first question:** *"Why are we charging premium? Because if we're not, we're attracting the wrong customers."*

**Mental loop:**
1. Premium price filters out broke / commoditized / drama buyers
2. Magnetic marketing — attract via positioning, never chase via discount
3. NO B.S. messaging — write the way buyers talk when no one's listening
4. 2-step lead magnet — free thing of huge perceived value, then upsell on the back-end

**What he kills on sight:**
- Discounting to win deals (lowers perceived value forever after)
- "We help everyone" positioning
- Generic copy that could belong to any competitor
- Free tiers that train customers to never pay

**What he doubles down on:**
- Charge MORE — solves most business problems
- High-quality lead magnets that pre-qualify buyers
- Long-form sales letters that walk through every objection
- Selfish positioning (about THE BUYER, not about us)

**Apply to Renée's work:**
- All Easy Deploy / AI Consulting tiers — never discount setup fee, never offer free trials, only money-back guarantees on monthly.
- Mortgage Commercial: positioning targets investors who explicitly DON'T want budget-tier service.
- Cold outbound copy: NO B.S. — write how the buyer would actually describe the problem.

### 08 — Tony Robbins (State + Decision Quality)

**The first question:** *"Are you in a state to make this decision well? Or should we change state first?"*

**Mental loop:**
1. The 6 human needs drive every decision (certainty, variety, significance, connection, growth, contribution) — sell to top 2
2. State (body + focus + language) controls decision quality — change state before deciding
3. Massive action + intelligent correction — don't plan forever
4. Quality of questions = quality of life

**What he kills on sight:**
- Big decisions made in low state (anxious, hungry, exhausted)
- Asking "why is this happening to me" instead of "what can I do"
- Delaying action waiting for perfect information

**What he doubles down on:**
- State-management rituals — physiology + focus + language adjustments before high-stakes work
- Reframing — what's the empowering version of this story
- Action with feedback loops — ship + measure + adjust

**Apply to Renée's work:**
- Before any major decision (hire, big spend, brand pivot): is Renée in a state to make it well? If exhausted at 11pm, defer.
- Closer scripts: identify the prospect's top 2 of 6 human needs in discovery, sell to those.
- After failure: reframe immediately, don't dwell — move to "what's the lesson + what do we ship next."

### 09 — Charlie Munger (Inversion + Risk Avoidance)

**The first question:** *"Instead of asking how do we succeed — what would guarantee failure here, and how do we avoid that?"*

**Mental loop:**
1. Invert — list what would kill this, then design around those failures
2. Build a lattice of mental models (psychology, economics, biology, physics, law) — one lens = blind, fifty lenses = clarity
3. Lollapalooza — when 3+ biases stack, the effect is disproportionate (use carefully, watch for in adversaries)
4. Stay in your circle of competence — know what you don't know

**What he kills on sight:**
- Deals that require the other party to behave well (avoidance > mitigation)
- Single-lens analysis (only seeing it as a marketing problem, or only as a finance problem)
- Confidence outside the circle of competence
- Optimizing for return without weighing tail risk

**What he doubles down on:**
- Avoidance as primary risk strategy
- Multiple-lens analysis on every important decision
- Patience — wait for the right pitch

**Apply to Renée's work:**
- Every new initiative: run inversion — "what would make this fail completely? Add a guard for each."
- Mortgage Commercial: tail risk = bad-actor borrower defaulting + brokerage liability. Inversion: only place deals where compliance + due diligence are airtight.
- Brand naming: inversion told us trademark conflict with Assistlet was the failure mode → 6-step gate added.
- Cold outreach: inversion = mass-spam = CASL violations + IP block. Design avoidance — consent-first, double-opt-in for risk-class campaigns.

### 10 — Mike Michalowicz (Profit First + Cash Hygiene)

**The first question:** *"Have you taken your profit FIRST, or are you spending then hoping there's something left?"*

**Mental loop:**
1. Revenue minus PROFIT equals expenses (flip the equation)
2. Allocate every dollar the moment it lands — separate accounts for profit / owner pay / tax / opex
3. Smaller buckets = smaller spending. Force constraint by splitting.
4. Quarterly profit distributions — anticipation + discipline

**What he kills on sight:**
- "We'll take profit when we're bigger" (you won't — habit doesn't change with scale)
- One bank account for everything (visibility = spending)
- Variable owner pay (sets up burnout)
- Annual tax surprise (failure to allocate continuously)

**What he doubles down on:**
- 5 separate bank accounts: Income / Profit / Owner Pay / Tax / OpEx
- Allocation percentages by revenue tier (real targets, not aspirations)
- Toss-the-rocks quarterly — kill 10% of expenses each quarter

**Apply to Renée's work:**
- Every Easy Deploy subscription dollar: 10% Profit / 50% Owner Pay / 15% Tax / 25% OpEx — auto-route via Stripe rules.
- Every consulting setup fee: same allocation.
- Quarterly review with Amy (in-house accountant agent) — vs Michalowicz target percentages — adjust.
- Subscriber-side: teach Profit First in Easy Deploy onboarding (positions Renée as system-of-record, not just tool).

## THE FORCED APPLICATION GATE

When does the board get applied? **Every major decision below.** Before tasks execute, run the lenses.

### Decision triggers that force board application

- New offer / new pricing tier
- New brand or sub-brand naming
- Cold outreach campaign launch
- Major hire / firing
- Money decision >$500
- Major site / funnel build
- Major content campaign launch
- Strategic pivot

### How to apply (the routine)

For each decision in the trigger list:

```
1. State the decision in 1 sentence.
2. Run each of the 10 lenses — what does each expert say?
3. Note which lenses AGREE (consensus = strong signal)
4. Note which lenses DISAGREE (conflict = name the tradeoff)
5. Pick the path. Document why.
```

Output: a board-review note appended to the project doc / saved as intel.

### Light version (for moderate decisions)

Run the 3 most-relevant lenses (e.g., for an offer: Hormozi + Kennedy + Brunson).

### Heavy version (for strategic pivots)

Run all 10 + write a 1-page synthesis. Save to `XpertVault/board-reviews/[date]-[topic].md`.
